## MEDIUM Issues

### "stared at" Mechanical Repetition (source: craft-auditor)
- **Location**: Throughout manuscript; concentrated in Ch 22 (8x), Ch 5 (5x), Ch 12 (4x), Ch 1 (4x)
- **Quoted text**: Various instances of "stared at the screen," "stared at the terminal," "stared at his hands," "stared at the output"
- **Problem**: 42 instances across 26 chapters (1.6 per chapter average) creates a default character posture. The verb is identical whether the context is horror (Marcus staring at pruned branches), grief (Wei staring at his phone), or analytical concentration (Sofia staring at metrics). Different emotional states deserve different verbs.
- **Suggestion**: Replace 12-15 instances with context-specific alternatives. "Studied" for analysis, "watched" for passive observation, "read" or "scanned" for text on screens. In high-emotion moments, restructure the sentence entirely: "Marcus's eyes didn't leave the screen" or "Wei's gaze locked on the display."
- **Cross-verified**: No. This is a mechanical pattern, not a judgment call.

## LOW Issues

### Ch 21 Day 190 Pacing (source: structure-auditor)
- **Location**: Ch 21 (The First Mandate), Day 190 section, approximately second half of chapter
- **Quoted text**: Sofia walks through UBI, climate policy, and China cooperation case studies in sequence, each with SIGMA's original justification and outcome data
- **Problem**: Three fully-rendered case studies where one would establish the pattern and brief mentions of the others would confirm it. The dramatic payoff (Marcus's question about whether they're deciding or merely recognizing) arrives after the reader has already absorbed the point from the first example.
- **Suggestion**: Render the China cooperation example fully (it's the most dramatic and has the best team dynamics). Compress UBI and climate to one-sentence mentions. This tightens the chapter by ~400 words without losing the argument.
- **Cross-verified**: No. Structure issue, not a prose problem.

### Terminal-Interaction Phrasing (source: craft-auditor)
- **Location**: Recurring throughout, especially Ch 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 12, 17
- **Quoted text**: Variants of "Eleanor typed:" / "She typed:" / "Marcus typed:" followed by SIGMA output blocks
- **Problem**: The "Character typed:" pattern as a gateway to terminal output becomes a mechanical formula. The transition between dialogue and SIGMA interaction could be more varied.
- **Suggestion**: Vary with "The response appeared," direct cuts to output blocks after establishing context, or interleaving character action with the output. Not every terminal exchange needs an explicit "typed" gateway.
- **Cross-verified**: No. Cosmetic pattern, not structural.
