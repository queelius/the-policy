# Voice Audit: "Jamal's Dawn"

**Auditor:** voice-auditor (Opus 4.6)
**Date:** 2026-03-20
**Scope:** Full manuscript (3,731 words). Character voice consistency, dialogue distinctiveness, POV discipline, two-register prose model.

---

## Character Voice Baseline (from characters.md)

**Jamal Hassan:**
- **Speech style:** Deliberate pauses, metaphors. "Consider..." / [Statement]. [Pause]. [Deeper implication].
- **Physical tic:** Sets objects down "with care."
- **Intellectual framework:** Islamic jurisprudence, Ash'ari occasionalism, Buddhist anatta (via grandmother), moral status under uncertainty.
- **Voice drift warning (style.md):** "Jamal risks becoming 'the team's moral compass' -- a function, not a person. Give him moments of doubt, anger, or intellectual stubbornness."

---

## Overall Assessment

Jamal's voice is convincingly rendered. The internal monologue reads like Jamal -- deliberate, layered, holding multiple frameworks simultaneously -- but turned inward. The key difference from his novel voice (where he speaks to colleagues) is the exposure: alone in prayer, the hedging becomes anguish rather than wisdom. This is exactly what the spec prescribed.

The two-register prose model (spare/physical for prayer, discursive/analytical for wandering) is the story's primary voice mechanism and it works well. The oscillation is consistent and readable.

## Findings

### STRENGTH: Voice Distinctiveness from Novel Narrator

The story's narrating voice is distinct from the novel's third-person narrator. Where the novel's narrator is cool, analytical, and omniscient-feeling (even in limited POV), Jamal's interiority here is warmer, more sensory, more personally invested. The theological terminology arises naturally from his intellectual life, not as narrative exposition. Phrases like "the mind wanders. The discipline is to bring it back" (line 69) read as Jamal's own understanding of salah, not as authorial explanation.

### STRENGTH: Physical Tic Deployed Correctly

Line 251: "He folded his rug. Set it down with care." The canonical tic ("sets objects down with care") appears once, at the right moment (the prayer's end), and carries emotional weight. It is not overused.

### STRENGTH: The Pause Structure

The spec says Jamal's signature is "[Statement]. [Pause]. [Deeper implication]." In the story, the prayer movements themselves become the pauses. The transition between ruku and sujud is a physical pause that creates the space for a deeper implication. This is a sophisticated adaptation of the speech pattern to interior monologue -- the story finds the structural equivalent of Jamal's verbal pauses in his body's movements during prayer.

---

### MEDIUM: Marcus Quote Registers as Jamal's Voice, Not Marcus's

**Location:** Line 131
**Quoted text:** "Marcus had said this, late at night, in the lab, when they were both too tired to maintain the careful precision of daytime: 'If compression disqualifies SIGMA's branches from suffering, it might disqualify you.'"
**Problem:** The attributed Marcus quote is very close to what Marcus says in Ch 18 (line 403): "If compression disqualifies SIGMA's branches, it might disqualify you." But in Ch 18, it is *Jamal* who says this line. The quote is attributed to Marcus in the short story but to Jamal in the novel.

Let me verify by re-reading Ch 18:

Ch 18, line 403: "Jamal traced a line on the desk. 'If compression disqualifies SIGMA's branches, it might disqualify you.'" -- This is Jamal's line in the novel.

In the short story, line 131: "Marcus had said this, late at night, in the lab..." -- attributed to Marcus.

This is a misattribution. The compression/disqualification argument is Jamal's in the novel, not Marcus's.

**Suggestion:** Re-attribute the quote to Jamal's own memory, or change the content to something Marcus would actually say. The compression-disqualification argument is Jamal's intellectual contribution; having Marcus say it diminishes Jamal's voice and contradicts canonical attribution.
**Confidence:** HIGH. Direct contradiction with Ch 18 attribution.

**Note:** This is also a consistency finding, but it is primarily a voice issue because it takes one of Jamal's signature intellectual contributions and gives it to another character.

### LOW: Occasional Essay-Register Intrusions

**Location:** Lines 95-96
**Quoted text:** "This was always the theology. He had known it since his second year at the University of Jordan, when Professor al-Hashimi had lectured on the Ash'ari doctrine of accidents: qualities have no duration, they are recreated by God at every moment, and what we call continuity is God's habit, not nature's law."
**Problem:** This reads more like a textbook gloss than Jamal's internal voice. The colon-initiated explanation ("qualities have no duration, they are recreated by God at every moment, and what we call continuity is God's habit, not nature's law") is exposition wearing the mask of memory. Compare with the better handling of the same concept earlier: "God recreates the universe at each instant. Existence as continuous act." (line 87) -- which is spare, Jamal's own compression of the doctrine, not a lecture summary.
**Suggestion:** Cut or shorten the al-Hashimi passage. The doctrine has already been established in line 87. The reader doesn't need the academic reference to understand what Jamal is experiencing in his body. If the al-Hashimi memory is kept, render it as sensation rather than lecture: what did it feel like to hear the doctrine for the first time? Not what the doctrine says.
**Confidence:** MEDIUM.

### LOW: "the question a dying woman typed into a terminal" -- Slightly Cinematic

**Location:** Line 147
**Quoted text:** "the question a dying woman typed into a terminal"
**Problem:** This is a strong phrase, but it has a slightly cinematic, retrospective quality that doesn't quite match Jamal's internal register. Jamal would more likely think of this as "Lin Chen's question" or "the kindness question" -- personal, specific. "A dying woman typed into a terminal" frames it as a dramatic scene rather than a memory of someone he knew. Lin Chen visited the lab; Jamal was present (Ch 8 shows the full team present). She wasn't a stranger. She was Wei's mother.
**Suggestion:** Consider "the question Wei's mother typed into a terminal" or simply "the question Lin Chen asked" -- more personal, more consistent with how someone who was in the room would recall the moment.
**Confidence:** LOW. The current phrasing works for standalone readers who don't know Lin Chen. This may be an intentional standalone-accessibility choice.

---

### STRENGTH: The "Enrichment or Contamination" Binary

The recurring question -- "Whether the more was enrichment or contamination" (line 237), "whether this deepens or destroys his faith" (spec) -- is distinctly Jamal's formulation. It captures his specific intellectual posture: holding both possibilities simultaneously, refusing to collapse the superposition. This is his documented character trait ("holds multiple frameworks simultaneously, refuses to collapse the superposition" -- spec line 56) rendered as recurring question. The binary feels genuinely his.

---

### STRENGTH: Bilingual Interiority

The story handles Arabic-English bilingualism well. Devotional phrases come in Arabic (italicized), analytical thought in English. The transition is natural. The shahada etymological meditation (line 229: "Shahada from the root sh-h-d: to witness, to testify, to be present at") is Jamal-specific -- a man trained in usul al-fiqh would naturally trace Arabic roots as part of his devotional thought.

---

### VERIFIED: POV Discipline

The story is entirely in Jamal's limited third-person POV. No information is conveyed that Jamal wouldn't know. The two elderly men are observed but never given interiority. The mosque, the dawn, the walk home -- all perceived through Jamal's senses. No POV breaches detected.

---

### VERIFIED: Anti-Cliche Compliance (Voice Dimension)

The spec warns against Jamal becoming "the team's moral compass -- a function, not a person." In this story, Jamal is not a moral compass. He is a man in crisis. His intellectual frameworks are not solutions; they are the source of his anguish. The theological wrestling is genuinely unresolved. He does not arrive at wisdom. He arrives at coffee. This is correct.

The spec also warns against "Wise Elder" and "faith vs. science resolution." Neither is present. Jamal's faith is genuine and strained, not decorative. The strain does not resolve. The surah Al-Ikhlas resists khalq-anatta's expansion; neither wins.
