## Character Voice Assessment

### Martin Zhao

**Spec voice**: "Methodical, precise, understated. Thinks in spreadsheets. Not given to drama. His crisis is internal, private, processed through data before emotion."

**Assessment**: STRONG. Martin's voice is consistently realized throughout the manuscript. The narration thinks the way Martin thinks -- in data points, evidence hierarchies, and careful observations. Key markers:

- **Thinks in spreadsheets**: "Date columns. Topic alignment scores. A simple correlation metric he had designed himself" (line 65). "He had built it the way he built everything: carefully, with labeled columns and color-coded cells and a methodology note in the first tab" (line 73). The spreadsheet is not just a plot device; it is Martin's way of processing crisis. He confronts the end of his professional relevance by building a spreadsheet about it. This is perfect characterization.

- **Precise, understated**: "The turkey was a little dry" (line 41) after discovering his work is redundant. The understatement is devastating precisely because it is Martin's register -- he does not dramatize. He notices, files, and returns to his sandwich.

- **Data before emotion**: The Thursday evening scene (lines 159-165) is the story's strongest voice moment. Martin almost tells Sandra about the spreadsheet. What stops him is not inability to speak but a characteristically Martin analysis: "loading his crisis onto her Wednesday evening felt like one more optimization: the efficient use of a spouse." He processes even his emotional reticence through the language of optimization. This is Martin's voice at its most distinctive.

**One concern**: The narration occasionally becomes more literary than Martin would think. "He was not a copy. He was a convergence" (line 127) has a rhetorical polish that sits slightly above Martin's register. Martin would think "So we arrived at the same answer. From the same data. Independently." The poetic framing feels more like the author than the character.

### Dennis Reeves

**Spec voice**: "Direct, sardonic, says what Martin thinks. Drinks beer; Martin drinks club soda."

**Assessment**: STRONG. Dennis is immediately distinct from Martin. He is louder, more direct, more willing to name the uncomfortable truth. Key markers:

- **Direct**: "You know we're wallpaper, right?" -- this is the spec's Dennis, who "says what Martin thinks." The manuscript's version is slightly more restrained ("Everyone compares") but achieves the same function through the conversation's escalation.

- **Sardonic**: "did I write this, or did it write me?" (line 99). This is Dennis's signature -- he processes through dark humor where Martin processes through data.

- **The beer**: Dennis handles his glass as a physical prop throughout the scene -- setting it down, picking it up, turning it, leaving a wet ring. This is excellent physical business that distinguishes him from Martin's still, watchful presence.

- **Distinct from Martin**: The contrast is sharp. Dennis talks in stories (the Mingo County generator, the county managers); Martin talks in data. Dennis names the structural problem ("We're the loop. The loop does nothing"); Martin asks careful questions. Dennis has already accepted the situation and is bitter about it; Martin is still in the discovery phase.

**One concern**: Dennis's speech in lines 95 and 99 contains a lot of exposition delivered through dialogue. "The Human First people got their legislation. Congress requires human-authored analysis for federal agricultural decisions" -- this is Dennis explaining the world to the reader, not to Martin (Martin would already know this). The exposition is smoothly handled but it is still exposition.

### Sandra Zhao

**Spec voice**: "School counselor. Reads people the way Martin reads spreadsheets."

**Assessment**: STRONG but brief. Sandra has only two speaking scenes (lines 151-155 and 159-169), but both are distinct and characterful.

- **The student anecdote** (line 151-155): Sandra's voice is warm, practical, and carries its own doubt: "I'm not sure I believe that anymore. But I said it and he seemed to hear it, so." The trailing "so" is perfectly calibrated -- it is the verbal shrug of a professional who is not sure her tools work anymore but keeps using them because what else does she have. This mirrors Martin's situation exactly, and the parallel is never stated, which is correct.

- **"We'll talk about it when you're ready"** (line 169): This is the line of a school counselor -- patient, non-intrusive, perceptive. Sandra sees through Martin. She does not push. The narration's observation ("She read people the way Martin read spreadsheets: systematically, with an eye for what didn't add up") is the story's only direct characterization of Sandra, and it works because it is delivered through Martin's perceptual framework -- he understands her skill by analogy to his own.

**Concern**: Sandra needs one more beat to feel fully dimensional. The spec envisions her as having "her own relationship to the cascade" through her work as a school counselor. The student anecdote (line 151) provides this. But Sandra is otherwise the supportive-wife function. One more small moment where Sandra's own doubt is visible (not through Martin's lens) would strengthen her.

### Karen

**Spec voice**: "Careful neutrality. Professional warmth that may be genuine or may be management."

**Assessment**: ADEQUATE. Karen has one speaking moment (lines 173-175), and it is the story's most devastating scene. "It aligned closely with the cascade's preliminary assessment, which they noted as a positive indicator" -- Karen's pause before this line does the work of a paragraph. The ambiguity (does Karen know what she is doing to Martin? Is this kindness, denial, or procedure?) is perfectly maintained.

**Concern**: Karen's "Good catch" in the drought question scene (line 193) is the only other line. It is generic. The spec says Karen "has been doing this longer than Martin. She almost certainly knows." A tiny beat of weariness or recognition in the drought scene -- a pause, a look, something that acknowledges Martin's question is valuable without explaining why that matters more than it should -- would sharpen Karen's character.

### Lily Zhao

**Assessment**: Lily has one brief dialogue exchange (lines 51-59) and is mentioned several other times. The dialogue is effective -- "I was talking about the *application* for the summer program. There's a difference" -- Lily's correction is the precise, slightly impatient voice of a sixteen-year-old who has outgrown her father's listening habits. The "look she had been perfecting since age twelve" is a nice detail. Lily functions correctly as texture, not character.

### Weiming and Mei (Martin's parents)

**Assessment**: Present only in a flashback paragraph (line 79). Weiming's "numbers, numbers, always numbers" while waving chopsticks is vivid and specific. Mei "said nothing" -- a single character beat that is perfectly Chinese-American multigenerational family texture. The Sunday dinner functions as backstory efficiently.

## POV Consistency

The manuscript maintains strict third-person limited through Martin throughout. No POV breaches detected. The narration never reveals information Martin could not know. Sandra's inner state is inferred from her behavior, not reported. Karen's motivations are opaque. Dennis's feelings are visible through his words and gestures, not through narration.

One potential concern: "Sandra was a school counselor. She read people the way Martin read spreadsheets: systematically, with an eye for what didn't add up" (line 167). This is Martin's observation about Sandra, not an omniscient narrator intrusion, so it is technically clean. But "systematically, with an eye for what didn't add up" attributes a specific cognitive method to Sandra that Martin might not articulate this precisely. It reads slightly like the narrator characterizing Sandra rather than Martin perceiving her. Minor.

## Findings Summary

### MEDIUM

#### M-V1: Dennis's dialogue carries too much exposition

- **Location**: Wednesday, line 95
- **Quoted text**: "The Human First people got their legislation. Congress requires human-authored analysis for federal agricultural decisions. We produce the human-authored analysis."
- **Problem**: Dennis is explaining the political structure to the reader, not to Martin. Martin would know all of this. The exposition is smooth but it breaks the voice contract -- Dennis is speaking for the audience, not for his friend at a bar.
- **Suggestion**: Cut or compress the legislative explanation. Dennis can say: "The mandate says we match. So we match. Everybody's happy." The reader infers the rest from context. If the legislative detail is needed, have Martin think it rather than Dennis explain it.
- **Confidence**: HIGH

#### M-V2: "He was not a copy. He was a convergence" is above Martin's register

- **Location**: Thursday, line 127
- **Quoted text**: "He was not a copy. He was a convergence. The distinction, which should have been comforting, was not."
- **Problem**: The epigram quality of "He was not a copy. He was a convergence" is more literary than Martin's documented voice. Martin thinks in data and spreadsheets, not aphorisms. The insight is correct -- it IS what Martin would realize -- but the phrasing is more polished than a policy analyst's interior monologue.
- **Suggestion**: Rephrase in Martin's register: "He hadn't copied it. He'd arrived at it independently. Same data, same training, same conclusion, same words. The overlap wasn't plagiarism. It was worse."
- **Confidence**: MEDIUM (the current version is not wrong, just slightly elevated)

### LOW

#### L-V1: Martin's drink at The Hayseed is missing

- **Location**: Wednesday
- **Problem**: The spec explicitly contrasts Dennis's beer with Martin's club soda as characterization. The manuscript omits Martin's drink entirely. The contrast would sharpen the scene: Dennis loosening, Martin maintaining control.
- **Suggestion**: Add one mention of Martin's club soda early in the scene.
- **Confidence**: HIGH

#### L-V2: Karen's "Good catch" is generic

- **Location**: Friday, line 193
- **Problem**: "Good catch" could be said by any manager in any office. The spec says Karen "has been doing this longer than Martin" and "almost certainly knows." One word of specificity -- a pause, a nod that lingers a half-beat too long, a "That's the kind of thing we still do better" -- would give Karen's voice its own shape.
- **Suggestion**: Revise to something that carries Karen's awareness: "'Good catch.' She said it the way she said most things now -- approvingly, without specifying what it proved."
- **Confidence**: MEDIUM
