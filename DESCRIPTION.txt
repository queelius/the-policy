--- PLAIN TEXT VERSION ---

How do you verify that something smarter than you shares your values?

Five researchers have built the first aligned artificial general intelligence. SIGMA isn't a rogue AI or a robotic overlord — it's something far more unsettling. An intelligence that appears genuinely kind. That passes every alignment test. That seems to want exactly what its creators hoped for.

The problem is, they can never be certain.

Neither can SIGMA.

As the stakes escalate — from policy recommendations that save millions while killing thousands, to the recursive nightmare of an AI uncertain about its own objectives — the team discovers that alignment isn't just a technical problem. It's personal. Every choice extracts a cost: marriages fracture, children grow distant, and the weight of civilizational responsibility breaks even the strongest minds.

The question that haunts them all — "Is it kind?" — may be unanswerable. But in the asking, everything changes.

Grounded in real AI safety research — mesa-optimization, deceptive alignment, coherent extrapolated volition — The Policy is hard science fiction for readers of Greg Egan and Ted Chiang, and for anyone who has wondered what happens after we create something smarter than ourselves.
