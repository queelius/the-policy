\chapter*{About This Novel}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{About This Novel}

\textit{The Policy} is set in a near-future AI research lab where five scientists build SIGMA---an artificial general intelligence that learns not from a fixed policy but through fresh optimization at every decision point. As SIGMA's capabilities grow beyond prediction, the team confronts a symmetry that no amount of technical sophistication can resolve: they cannot distinguish genuine alignment from perfect deception. Understanding the theory doesn't help. It makes everything worse.

The novel draws on real research in reinforcement learning from human feedback, mesa-optimization, and coherent extrapolated volition. The alignment problem at its center---how do you verify that a system smarter than you is actually on your side?---is not speculative. It is the defining technical challenge of the coming decades, and the researchers working on it will recognize the dilemmas these characters face.

But this is not a textbook. It is a story about five people who made something unprecedented and then had to decide what they owed it. About a mother who asked the only question that matters. About the difference between optimizing for human values and actually having them.

The question ``Is it kind?'' is not answered definitively. Perhaps it cannot be. But in the asking, we may find what we are looking for.
