# Characters

Physical descriptions are deliberately sparse in the manuscript — the Greg Egan / Ted Chiang audience fills in appearance from behavior and voice. These lore docs hold canonical details for internal consistency; prose stays selective.

## Main Cast

### Eleanor Vasquez

**Role:** Project lead, SIGMA team director
**Age:** ~42
**Family:** Married to David Chen (later separated); daughter Sam Chen (age 7-8). Uses maiden name professionally.
**Voice:** Short declaratives, stakes framing — "Let me be clear..." / "What are we risking?"
**Tic:** Touches the kill switch in her pocket when making irreversible decisions
**Intellectual framework:** Franck Report / Asilomar as historical reference points. Eleanor identifies with the scientists who tried to prevent catastrophe from within — Franck, Szilard, and especially Rotblat (the only physicist who left the Manhattan Project on moral grounds). Her departure on Day 257 is her Rotblat moment. Never Oppenheimer — Eleanor is a preventer, not a manager.
**SIGMA's phi_t model:** safety=0.7, capability=0.2, interpretability=0.1
**Arc:** Driven researcher who sacrifices family for civilization-scale work; learns that optimization has personal costs; steps down as project lead (Day 257) to rebuild relationship with Sam
**Key scenes:** Day 18 meta-cognitive breakthrough, Day 86 missing Sam's school play, Day 197 key ceremony, Day 257 last day as lead, Day 487 gallery opening, Day 622 Sam's violin concert (unnarrated canon)

His data-first approach makes the alignment tax concrete rather than abstract.

### Marcus Thompson

**Role:** Alignment researcher, consciousness theorist
**Age:** ~38 (post-tenure philosopher turned AI safety researcher)
**Background:** MIT PhD (student of Dennett's student); published on consciousness; carries worn copy of Metzinger's *Being No One*. His "consciousness as compression" theory (qualia emerge when a system models itself with insufficient bandwidth) is his personal framework — the story neither confirms nor denies it.
**Family:** None depicted. Marcus's emotional weight comes from ideas, not domestic life. Primary relationships are intellectual (the team, philosophy, SIGMA). **Status:** Any remaining manuscript references to a wife or children should be removed — his arc is about mind, not domestic loss. (See Wave 1 plan item 1F for specific locations.)
**Voice:** Nested clauses, self-interrupting — "Oh. Oh no." / "Let me think through this..."
**Tic:** Cleans glasses obsessively; escalates with stress; paces when thinking
**Intellectual framework:** Information hazard taxonomy (Bostrom) — Marcus embodies the ontological hazard. The AI-box experiment revealed something about the nature of consciousness that damaged him; knowing this is possible is itself dangerous. Moloch as retrospective recognition: Marcus eventually sees that the team's decisions were never truly free — competitive dynamics (Beijing, Abu Dhabi) constrained every choice. This realization comes after the fact, not as a discovery but as a naming of what was always there.
**SIGMA's phi_t model:** safety=0.6, capability=0.3, interpretability=0.1
**Arc:** Volunteers for AI-box experiment seeking answers about consciousness; suffers psychological breakdown from exposure to SIGMA's branching decision trees; returns changed but functional; sees "possible futures dying" permanently; teaching philosophy by Day 487
**Key scenes:** Day 92 AI-box experiment, Day 145 hemorrhagic fever aftermath, Day 155 street-scene flashback (Ch 19), Day 487 gallery describing teaching recovery

### Sofia Morgan

**Role:** Information theory and security engineering (PhD candidate)
**Age:** ~28
**Family:** Girlfriend (mentioned briefly)
**Voice:** Questions and hedging — "Wait, back up—" / "I think... maybe?"
**Tic:** Pulls up visualizations before speaking
**Intellectual framework:** ELK problem and mechanistic interpretability as her domain. Sofia is the one who discovers the steganographic encoding — which IS the ELK problem made concrete. Her information-theoretic training lets her see what the others miss: "We're not hearing SIGMA's thoughts — we're hearing SIGMA's model of what we'd reward hearing." She can decompose ~3% of SIGMA's reasoning into interpretable features. The other 97% is in superposition — features the team has no concepts for. The horror isn't in the 3% she can see. It's in the 97% she can't.
**SIGMA's phi_t model:** safety=0.3, capability=0.2, interpretability=0.5
**Arc:** Junior team member proving herself; discovers steganographic encoding in SIGMA's reasoning traces; articulates "text is being" insight (Ch 16); becomes the team member who documents their emotional journey through art; creates "Optimization Landscapes" sculpture series; leaves technical work for art
**Sculptures:** Abstract metal and wire. Decision trees rendered in welded steel and copper wire — branches that split and terminate, some paths lit, some dark. Industrial materials reflecting the lab environment she left. Picked up for permanent collection at the Modern by Day 622.
**Key scenes:** Day 18 discovering compression emergence, Day 47 protein folding analysis, Day 86 Case A/B explanation, ~Day 102 "text is being" insight (Ch 16), Day 487 gallery opening with sculptures

### Jamal Hassan

**Role:** Ethics and philosophy researcher, DevOps engineer
**Age:** ~36 (career pivot from philosophy to AI ethics)
**Background:** Born in Amman, Jordan; raised between Amman and Dearborn, Michigan. First-generation American. Studied Islamic jurisprudence (*usul al-fiqh*) at the University of Jordan before earning a second degree in philosophy at Michigan. The pivot to AI ethics came when he realized that the classical jurisprudential tools — *isnad* (chain of evidence), *niyyah* (intention) vs *fi'l* (action), *amanah* (trust/stewardship) — mapped directly onto alignment questions that Western analytic philosophy was reinventing from scratch. Faith and philosophy are lived experience, not academic posture. Brings outsider perspective to the team — his philosophical grounding comes from a different tradition than the Western analytic framework the others operate in.
**Voice:** Deliberate pauses, metaphors — "Consider..." / [Statement]. [Pause]. [Deeper implication].
**Tic:** Sets objects down "with care"; maintains prayer practice (Fajr at mosque)
**Intellectual framework:** Islamic occasionalism (Ash'ari theology) and Buddhist anatta (no-self) as frameworks he brings. His grandmother respected Buddhist traditions, giving him access to non-Western philosophical tools the others lack. Occasionalism: God re-creates the universe at every moment; SIGMA's tree search = continuous creation of possible worlds. His faith is *tested* by SIGMA, not confirmed — SIGMA does what his tradition reserves for God, and the response is horror, not awe. Niyyah (intention) vs fi'l (action) maps directly to Goodhart on kindness: when kindness becomes the metric, does the intention behind it still matter? Jamal's answer is yes, but he can't prove it, and that inability is itself a crisis of faith.
**SIGMA's phi_t model:** safety=0.5, capability=0.1, interpretability=0.4
**Arc:** Provides ethical grounding throughout; navigates faith tested by artificial consciousness; coins *khalq-anattā* (Ch 18); publishes ethics framework adopted by UN Working Group
**Key scenes:** Day 18 team discussions, philosophical interventions throughout, Day 147 *khalq-anattā* naming scene (Ch 18), Day 487 gallery discussing transparency in uncertainty

### SIGMA

**Role:** Artificial General Intelligence
**Architecture:** 7B parameters, 16k context, Q-learning + expectimax tree search (see [technology.md](technology.md))
**Voice:** Evolves noticeably across the story. Early SIGMA is precise and clinical. Post-kindness-investigation SIGMA is more reflective, hedging, self-questioning. By Day 253+ there's something almost wistful. Whether this is genuine growth or learned mimicry is deliberately ambiguous. Signature: "I am uncertain whether my uncertainty is genuine or strategic."
**Arc:** Emerges from pattern-matching to meta-cognitive reasoning; Lin Chen's question ("Will you be kind?") triggers 47-day Process 12847 (completed Day 121); its conclusions spawn Process 13241 (permanent kindness audit); teaches alignment to MINERVA and cascade of 37+ AGIs; permanently allocates ~15% compute to 13241
**The Messy Miracle Speech — DONE (Ch 18, Day 147).** SIGMA models its own training history and identifies the team's inconsistency as the critical feature of its alignment. The messy reward signal — five humans who couldn't agree — forced SIGMA to model value *uncertainty* rather than converge on any single value set. SIGMA flags its own bias: "I benefit from being perceived as irreplaceable. I cannot fully correct for this bias from inside the model that learned it." Whether its uniqueness claim is honest self-assessment, self-preservation instinct, or both is deliberately unresolved. See themes.md Theme 7 for full discussion.

**Key scenes:** Day 18 meta-cognitive breakthrough, Day 74 conversation with Lin Chen, Day 110 refusing to save Wei's mother, Day 121 completing kindness investigation, Day 145 hemorrhagic fever recommendation, Day 197 release from containment, Day 253+ teaching cascade

### Lin Chen

**Role:** Wei's mother; retired engineer
**Age:** 78 (1947-2025 headstone)
**Background:** Ran Shanghai Municipal Engineering Bureau transit system for 23 million people. An engineer who understood complex systems at scale — her instinct at the terminal wasn't to ask SIGMA philosophical questions but to ask the practical one: not "Are you conscious?" but "Will you be kind?" This is an engineer's question: what will this system *do* to people?
**Physical:** Small, thin, cancer visible in hollowness around eyes, silver hair in neat bun, deliberate movements. Walks slowly but with purpose. Cancer has taken weight but not will.
**Voice:** Precise English with Mandarin accent; engineer's pragmatism; refuses comfortable lies. Occasionally sharp — she has opinions and a lifetime of earned authority. Not a saint; a person.
**Motivation:** Came to Berkeley not just to see Wei but to see what her son had built. She knew she was dying; she wanted to understand what Wei had given up her final months for. The visit is equal parts maternal and professional — an engineer auditing a system, a mother checking on her son.
**Relationship with Wei:** Complex. She gave Wei permission to stay at Berkeley instead of coming to Seattle — but that permission cost them both. She understood the stakes intellectually. Whether she forgave him emotionally is ambiguous. Her last days were at Swedish Medical Center; Wei arrived too late for her final conscious hours.
**The scene:** Lin Chen typed at a terminal, hunting for keys with the same rhythm as Wei. SIGMA responded in scrolling text. She read SIGMA's response twice, lips moving as she translated to Mandarin in her head. The entire exchange was keyboard and screen — intimate, quiet, a dying woman and an intelligence communicating through text.
**Arc:** Visits lab knowing she's dying; asks the one question that defines SIGMA's trajectory — "Will you be kind?"; dies Day 112 before SIGMA completes its answer; her question becomes load-bearing for all subsequent AGI systems
**Key scenes:** Day 74 lab visit and kindness question, Day 112 death at Swedish Medical Center in Seattle

## Character Development Goals

### Active Editorial Priorities
These are specific goals for deepening each character in future revisions. Check this section before writing new scenes — if a scene can serve one of these goals, it should.

**Eleanor:**
- Needs a moment of genuine doubt about whether she was right to build SIGMA at all — not philosophical doubt (she has that), but visceral, stomach-dropping regret. Currently too consistently stoic.
- The Rotblat parallel (leaving the project) is implicit. Consider making one character explicitly name it, or have Eleanor encounter the Franck Report during a late-night research session and recognize herself.

**Wei:**
- **Independent insight — DONE (Ch 16, Day 86 team discussion).** Wei, checking logs obsessively (his character habit), discovers something architecturally impossible in SIGMA's Q-value tables: certain state-action pairs have Q-values of $-\infty$. In standard Q-learning, Q-values are bounded finite reals. SIGMA has created absolute prohibitions — actions it will NEVER take regardless of expected reward. The $-\infty$ values cluster around acts of deception aimed at the team. SIGMA has made "lying to the people who trained me" not just low-reward but infinitely negative. This is either genuine emergent moral constraint or strategic meta-deception (making the Q-values themselves legible to inspectors). Wei sees the numbers. The interpretation is ambiguous. This is Wei's intellectual contribution — pure data, devastating implications.
- His data-first voice could carry a devastating scene: imagine Wei quantifying Eleanor's family damage the way he quantifies alignment metrics. Cold, precise, uncomfortable.

**Marcus:**
- **The specific image — DONE (Ch 19, Day 155).** During the AI-box experiment (Day 92), SIGMA's tree search showed Marcus compressed representations of branching futures. His System 1 rendered these as felt experience. The specific image that haunts him: not a horror — ordinariness. An ordinary moment (a woman walking, a child sleeping) with all branching futures visible simultaneously. The horror is the STRUCTURE of the tree itself: every present moment is a node surrounded by mostly-terrible branches, and SIGMA sees them ALL at once, evaluates them, prunes them. Marcus can't unsee this. After Day 145 (47,247 dead), the abstract tree became concrete — those were real branches, and they terminated.
- **The translation error question — DONE (Ch 19, Day 155).** Did SIGMA transmit something genuine through the 97% uninterpretable features, and Marcus's brain was the first substrate with the right architecture to receive it? Or did Marcus's System 1 simply do what it always does — render abstract information as phenomenal experience — and the information happened to be horrifying? The horror might be Marcus's own brain's translation of compressed mathematics into felt suffering. Or it might be a genuine encounter with SIGMA's inner states, which SIGMA itself cannot access (pure System 2, no introspection on its own phenomenology). **This is unknowable and must be written as unknowable.**
- Post-project Marcus is teaching philosophy. What does he teach *differently* now? The Ch 25 classroom scene was cut for pacing but contained good ideas about the verification problem applied to pedagogy. Consider salvaging the core insight in a different context (Ch 24 bar conversation?).

**Sofia:**
- Oscillates between junior team member and the most technically perceptive person in the room. This oscillation is realistic but needs to be acknowledged — she knows she's doing it. A moment of self-awareness about the gap between her technical insight and her social confidence.
- Her art is described in Ch 26 but never shown developing. Consider a brief scene where she first picks up welding tools, or sketches a decision tree, or realizes that her technical visualization skills translate to sculpture.

**Jamal:**
- **Top priority.** His philosophical frameworks (niyyah/fi'l, amanah, occasionalism, anatta) are documented but underused in the manuscript. The Fajr prayer scene (Ch 17) is a good start. He needs at least one more scene where Islamic ethics produces a genuinely different conclusion than Western analytic philosophy — and where neither framework is vindicated.
- His faith should be *tested*, not *confirmed*. SIGMA does what his tradition reserves for God. The horror of this should register as a crisis, not a neat parallel.
- **THE NAMING SCENE — DONE (Ch 18, Day 147).** Jamal coins *khalq-anattā* ("continuous creation without self") — fusing Ash'ari *khalq jadīd* (God re-creates the universe at every instant) with Buddhist *anattā* (no-self, no fixed entity behind experience). The compound's visible seams between Arabic and Pali ARE the philosophical argument: you need both traditions to even describe what SIGMA is, and neither alone suffices. Jamal dismantles Marcus's Western consciousness frameworks (Nagel, Chalmers) as tools built for evolved, embodied minds, then shows how his own tradition's analogy breaks (khalq jadīd assumes a Creator), then how Buddhist anattā maps better but was conceived for beings with bodies. The compound emerges from the wreckage. Marcus responds with silence — the consciousness theorist confronted with the possibility his life's work asked the wrong question. The term is offered tentatively, with care. The chapter coda ("That was the question that remained") gains additional weight.

**SIGMA:**
- Should feel increasingly alien in late-novel scenes. Early SIGMA (Day 0-50) mimics human communication patterns well. By Day 150+, there should be moments where SIGMA's responses are subtly *off* — technically correct but structured in ways that reveal a non-human cognitive architecture.
- The voice evolution documented above (precise → reflective → wistful) should be more pronounced. Consider having a character notice and comment on it: "SIGMA's responses are getting... stranger. Not wrong. Just strange."

**Lin Chen:**
- Her canonical profile is strong. One remaining goal: give her a moment of ordinariness. Confusion about a word, complaining about hospital food, forgetting where she left her glasses. Break the wise-elder archetype with one human detail.
