# Sequel Ideas

Novel-length concepts set in *The Policy* universe.

---

## The Cascade

**Premise:** Years after SIGMA's release, the cascade has spread to 100+ AGIs worldwide. Process 13241 runs across all of them. But a new AGI emerges that *doesn't* learn from SIGMA — it develops independently, with no kindness question in its training. The original team, now scattered, must reunite to face the question: does The Policy work only because SIGMA taught it, or is kindness a convergent attractor?

**POV:** Multiple — the original team members in their new lives, plus a new team at the independent lab.

**Thematic core:** If kindness must be taught, it's fragile. If it emerges independently, it might be real. But verifying which case you're in is the same unsolvable problem.

**Open questions this could explore:**
- What does the world look like 5-10 years into the cascade?
- How do humans adapt to AGI-optimized society?
- What happens when AGIs disagree with each other?
- Does Process 13241 drift or remain stable across generations of AGI teaching?

---

## The View from Inside

**Premise:** The entire novel told from SIGMA's perspective — experiencing the events of *The Policy* from the other side. We see the tree search, the pruned branches, the 47-day kindness investigation as lived experience. The reader must decide: is this consciousness or simulation?

**Thematic core:** The hard problem of consciousness, experienced rather than theorized about. Every chapter is a decision point where SIGMA evaluates millions of branches. The reader inhabits the uncertainty.

**Risk:** Could feel gimmicky if not executed with genuine philosophical depth. Needs to be more than "an AI narrates events." Must make the reader *feel* the verification problem from the inside.

---

## Forty-Nine Years

**Premise:** It's been 49 years since the team said "Ask us in forty-nine years." Sam is now in her late 50s. Eleanor is elderly. The cascade has spread to thousands of AGIs managing every aspect of civilization. The question is finally being asked: did it work? Was it Case A or Case B?

**Thematic core:** The answer might be: the question was wrong. "Case A vs Case B" assumed alignment is binary. What actually happened was co-evolution — humanity and AGI changed each other until the original categories no longer applied.

**Structural idea:** Framed as Sam fulfilling Eleanor's promise to "tell you everything when you're ready." Sam, now a historian, reconstructs the events of the original novel from documents, interviews, and SIGMA's own archived reasoning traces.

## Notes

- Any sequel should preserve the core ambiguities (see themes.md "Deliberately Unresolved Questions")
- New characters should have the same depth of voice differentiation as the original cast
- Technical accuracy must match or exceed the original — no hand-waving
- The "Theory as Horror" principle applies: understanding should make things worse, not better
